# Sentinel design notes

Reading: a cinematic product launch for engineering teams. Original art direction informed by Butter's physicality, typographic scale, open composition, and motion timing, without reusing its branding, assets, or layouts.

- DESIGN_VARIANCE: 9. Editorial compositions, a bespoke signal lens, spatial evidence, and a horizontal agent sequence.
- MOTION_INTENSITY: 8. Movement establishes causal relationships, incident states, and recovery. Reduced motion keeps the whole narrative available.
- VISUAL_DENSITY: 4. Large quiet sections frame detailed, interactive product examples.
- Foundation: Next.js, Tailwind v4, custom CSS tokens, Geist, JetBrains Mono, Phosphor icons.
- Palette: the Colour Hunt set — butter cream, sky blue, mauve, dusty brown — re-keyed onto the slate ground #5c7285 the user supplied, with the beige panels kept. The ground went dark at the user's direction and the theme turned over with it: what was a light page carrying dark ink is now a mid-dark page carrying light ink, so every text, mark and accent value that sits directly on the ground had to be re-solved, and only the panels kept the ink they had. The binding constraint is a number, and a harsher one than before. The ground sits at luminance 0.160, so AA needs ink at luminance 0.895 or above — a window 0.105 wide, against the previous ground's far wider band. A max-chroma search across that window returns its most saturated legal colour at chroma ≈ 0.17, so ground ink is necessarily near-white and no hue survives in it. That is why the ground ladder is flat — text, muted, mid and dim step down 4.95 → 4.72 → 4.57 → 4.52 — and why hierarchy could not be carried on the ground at all. It moved into the panels instead.
- The panels are now the only place hierarchy lives, and the separation got clearer rather than weaker. The beige #efe3c8 and #e6d7b4 sit at 3.93:1 and 3.51:1 against the slate — both above the 3:1 UI floor, where against the old light-blue ground they managed only 2.30:1 and 2.06:1. Their ink is unchanged and still steps 14.09 → 12.20 → 10.82 → 10.42 for text, muted, mid and dim, with rules and accent between 6.9 and 10.1. So the page reads as two planes: a low-contrast slate field where everything is white and near-white and nothing defers to anything else, punctuated by high-contrast beige panels where the full ladder is available. The flat ground is the price of the colour the user asked for; the panels absorbing all of the hierarchy is what makes that price payable. The header is the case that proves it. It paints nothing itself and is fixed at the top of the viewport while beige panels scroll under it, so it cannot inherit either plane's ink: both its nav pill and its brand plate are panel-plane elements in their own right, each carrying the beige and the dark ink rather than the ground's near-white, which would measure 1.26:1 on a panel passing beneath.
- Two planes means the token re-declaration has to be explicit, and this is the one non-obvious mechanical fact in the theme. `body { color: var(--text) }` resolves `--text` **at `body`**; a descendant inherits a finished colour, not a pending substitution. Re-declaring `--text` inside a panel therefore does nothing for text that merely inherited it — the panel rule must also assert `color: var(--text)` so the variable is resolved again in its own context. Custom properties do inherit, so one comma-separated selector list covering every panel and panel-descendant sets the whole subtree from a single rule. That list cannot reach the literal hexes in the stylesheets, and those are classified by the plane the browser proves they sit on rather than by their nesting, because the DOM crosses class-name boundaries (`.root-card-bottom` inside `.root-cause-card`, `.evidence-detail` inside `.evidence-section`).
- The accent splits by plane rather than by job, because one value cannot serve both. On the slate, #d7cfe6 is 3.32:1 — past the 3:1 boundary floor, so it works as a fill and as a mark — and `--accent-ink` #322545 reads 9.39:1 inside it. In the panels the same role is the deep plum #4d3c60 at 7.74:1, carrying the light label #f4ecda at 8.38:1. `--accent-text`, the accent-as-type value, flips with the plane too: #f6f4fa on the ground (4.58:1) and #322545 in the panels (11.10:1). Rules follow the same split: ground rules run light, #c9d7e2 at 3.40:1 and #d4e1ea at 3.75:1 — above the UI floor so they read as boundaries, deliberately below the 4.52:1 of the dimmest ground copy so a rule never competes with the words it separates — while panel rules are dark, #243a56 and #1e3252, at 9-10:1. Success and warning keep their jobs on both planes.
- The hairline rule flipped direction on the ground. Under the light page marks were darkened, because light-on-light is invisible; on the slate that relationship reverses again, so sheet lines, sparkline bars, status dots, the telemetry chart and the two 3D scenes' ground marks all went light. Marks are still held to the 3:1 UI floor rather than treated as ornament, because several of them (the sparkline, the status dot) carry state.
- The Three.js environment presets are keyed to the page, not to a mood: `studio` for the hero, a quieter `graphite` for section scenes. The environment is what the metal reflects, so a rig keyed to a world that is not on screen lights every polished surface from somewhere the viewer cannot see and the sculpture reads as a cut-out pasted onto the page. On the slate page that world is the slate field itself — #5c7285, with edge stops at #3d4c59 and mid-bands at #6f8798 and #54697a, and `graphite` a cooler #54697a with edges #33414d and #455667. The beige bands, the cream highlights and the white and plum blocks are kept from the beige work, because a mirror-metal object still needs some bright in what it mirrors to keep its form, and the slate field is dark enough that the near-black metal tints separate against it in a way the light ground never allowed. The lens core glows in the palette's brightest tone (#f7f0de) with its rim fading into the ground (#5c7285), the same ground-fade structure as before, and the sculpture's accent material is the lavender #d7cfe6 with the ambient light taking the ground itself, so the object's shadow side sits in the same world as the page behind it.
- Radius: 6px controls, 12px product surfaces, circles only for real signal nodes and icon controls.
- Theme: two planes on a dark ground, keyed to the user-supplied slate. The ground is the one input the panel ladder is derived against, and it is now darker than the panels rather than lighter; `color-scheme: dark` is set on the root (and the viewport `themeColor` is the ground) so form controls, scrollbars, and the browser chrome match the slate, with the panel rule overriding to `color-scheme: light` inside the beige surfaces so their own controls stay dark-on-light.
- Layers: base 0, visual content 1, floating evidence 2, sticky navigation 20, dialog 50, skip link 60.
- Motion ownership: GSAP owns section transforms and scroll timelines, including the scroll progress each scene-scoped Three.js canvas is driven by. Motion owns buttons, local card tilt, and state transitions. They never animate the same element.
- Three.js is scoped to three scenes: the hero signal lens, the evidence graph in `#root-cause`, and the lifecycle rail in `#platform`. All three are dynamically imported on capable desktop screens, each holds at most one WebGL context, and each keeps a static fallback — the hero's local WebP render, and the existing SVG connectors and route. The section scenes mount only within 1024px-and-up outside reduced motion, and only while near the viewport, so at most one is ever live.
- Layer and budget guardrails: `context-budget.ts` caps the app at 4 live WebGL contexts, well under the browser's own limit, so a scene that cannot acquire one declines to mount rather than being dropped mid-scroll. Section scenes sit in an absolutely-positioned layer beneath their DOM content and take no pointer events, so every button, focus stop, and accessible name stays with the DOM.
- User brief overrides skill defaults for three-line display typography, interactive product previews, simulated technical metadata, and state-based color.
- All incident data is illustrative. No backend credentials, source-code mutations, or production actions are involved.
